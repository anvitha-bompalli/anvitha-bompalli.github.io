Exploring Distributed Cognition

How does a restaurant's physical environment guide customer decisions? I conducted an observational study at Qdoba to explore how spatial layouts, visual cues, and employee interactions work together to support the ordering process.

Role

Researcher | Independent Project

Methods

Field Observation, Spatial Mapping, Interaction Analysis, Distributed Cognition

Framework

Distributed Cognition

Timeline

Fall 2024

The question

Ordering at a fast-casual restaurant involves multiple decisions and interactions in a short amount of time. I wanted to understand how the physical layout, menu design, and employee coordination distribute information throughout the ordering process, and where customers might experience confusion.

What I did

  1. Field Observation: Observed the ordering process in person, documenting customer-employee interactions, physical arrangements, and information artifacts through photographs and field notes.
  2. Spatial Mapping: Created a hand-drawn map of the restaurant's layout to examine how ingredient placement, menu boards, and employee positions support the ordering workflow.
  3. Interaction Analysis: Analyzed how information moves between customers, employees, and physical artifacts, focusing on task sequencing, role transitions, and decision-making.
  4. Usability Evaluation: Identified areas where visual information supported or disrupted the ordering experience and developed design recommendations based on these observations.

Key findings

  • Spatial organization supported an intuitive workflow. Ingredients were arranged in the same sequence as the overhead menu, helping customers navigate decisions while allowing employees to coordinate tasks efficiently.
  • Poor label visibility created unnecessary confusion. Labels for salsas and toppings were small and positioned near the bottom of the glass, making it difficult to identify ingredients during ordering.
  • Employee coordination reduced the need for explicit communication. Staff seamlessly transferred orders between stations, using the physical progression of the food as a signal for the next step.

Proposed improvements

  • Improve ingredient visibility: Place clear, readable labels directly above or beside each ingredient to help customers identify their options without additional clarification.
  • Preserve step-by-step guidance: Maintain the alignment between overhead menu sections and ingredient stations to support decision-making, particularly for first-time customers.

Information Systems Analysis of the 2020 U.S. Presidential Election

How do demographics, information systems, and predictive models shape our understanding of elections? We examined the 2020 U.S. presidential election as a complex information system, exploring stakeholder relationships, voting patterns, and the role of data in election forecasting.

Role

Researcher & Analyst

Tools

Miro

Focus

Demographic Analysis, Information Systems Mapping, Secondary Research, Election Forecasting

Timeline

Fall 2024

The question

Elections involve complex interactions between voters, campaigns, government agencies, media, and forecasting organizations. We wanted to understand how information moves through these systems, how demographic characteristics relate to voting patterns, and how researchers use historical and demographic data to predict election outcomes.

What I did

  1. Information Systems Mapping: Collaborated on social world models and data journeys to visualize the stakeholders, technologies, and information flows involved in the election process.
  2. Demographic Research: Examined U.S. Census data and published research to investigate how voter turnout and voting patterns varied across demographic groups.
  3. Predictive Model Analysis: Reviewed research on machine learning-based election forecasting to understand how demographic and historical voting data can be used to generate predictions.
  4. Research Synthesis: Combined findings from government reports, academic publications, and election analyses to identify patterns, information gaps, and limitations in available data.

Key findings

  • Demographics were important for understanding voting patterns. Research revealed differences in voter participation and candidate support across demographic groups, highlighting the importance of looking beyond overall election results.
  • Election data has important limitations. Demographic voting estimates often rely on surveys and sample-based studies rather than complete population-level records, introducing uncertainty into the analysis.
  • COVID-19 changed election information systems. The pandemic increased the importance of mail-in voting and created unusual conditions that complicated comparisons with previous elections and the interpretation of historical voting data.

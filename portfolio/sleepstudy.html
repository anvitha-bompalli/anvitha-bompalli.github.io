Effect of sleep on college students' lives

What factors influence sleep quality among college students? We combined qualitative interviews, survey research, and quantitative analysis to explore how academic demands, work, lifestyle habits, and stress relate to students' sleep.

Role

Researcher & Data Analyst

Tools

Qualtrics, Power BI

Methods

Qualitative Interviews, Survey Design, Quantitative Analysis, Data Visualization, Correlation Analysis

Timeline

Fall 2025

The question

College students balance academics, work, extracurricular activities, and personal responsibilities, all of which may influence their sleep. We wanted to understand which factors students perceived as affecting their sleep and whether measurable relationships existed between these aspects of their lives and their sleep quality.

What I did

  1. Exploratory Research: Reviewed existing literature and analyzed qualitative student interviews to identify factors influencing sleep and establish the foundation for our research.
  2. Survey Design & Data Collection: Translated qualitative themes into survey questions and distributed a Qualtrics survey using convenience, snowball, and voluntary sampling, collecting 119 responses.
  3. Data Preparation: Developed a composite Sleep Quality Score using four survey measures: sleep sufficiency, difficulty falling asleep, difficulty staying asleep, and feeling refreshed upon waking.
  4. Quantitative Analysis: Examined relationships between sleep quality and academic workload, employment, extracurricular involvement, and lifestyle habits using correlation analysis and descriptive statistics.
  5. Data Visualization: Created Power BI visualizations to communicate patterns in sleep quality, student behaviors, and perceived factors affecting sleep.

Key findings

  • Academic stress emerged as a major perceived influence. 65% of respondents reported that stress surrounding exams and assignments negatively affected their sleep.
  • Students recognized the academic importance of sleep. 83% strongly agreed that a good night's sleep helps them focus better academically, while 87% reported relying on caffeine to some extent for academic functioning.
  • Time commitments showed little linear relationship with sleep quality. Correlations between sleep quality and academic hours (r ≈ 0), work hours (r = 0.12), and extracurricular hours (r = 0.05) were negligible or very weak.
